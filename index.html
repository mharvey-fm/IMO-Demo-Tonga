About the Workshop

What the demonstration covers

Franmarine showcases commercially mature ROV inspection technology within the MTCC Pacific course, with the IMO TEST Biofouling PCU and the Marine and Ports Division (Ministry of Infrastructure).

🤖

Flying ROV — niche areas

Deep Trekker Pivot inspection of sea chests, gratings, thrusters, rudders, propellers, intakes, anodes and the waterline — including aluminium, fibreglass and composite hulls.

🧲

Hull crawler survey

Deep Trekker DT640 magnetic-tracked lane coverage of steel main hull plating, vertical sides, flats and bilge keels.

📋

Fouling Rating assessment

Live annotation in MarineStream against hull zones and niche areas, aligned with the 2023 IMO Biofouling Guidelines (MEPC.378(80)).

📡

Live demo at Faua Wharf

Real-time video projection to participants via MarineStream over Starlink — one demonstration day within 18–21 August 2026.

Built for a SIDS setting

Why this technology fits Tonga

Portable, self-contained and vessel-independent inspection that matches dispersed maritime activity and limited shore infrastructure — without a dry dock, slipway or crane.

🏝️

Wharf, small craft or shoreline

Operate from Faua Wharf or similar sites with no fixed shore facility — the same kit moves between locations and vessel sizes.

🚢

Commercial and recreational

Steel-hulled vessels use the crawler plus Pivot; smaller aluminium, fibreglass and composite craft are inspected contact-free with the Pivot alone.

📈

Evidence for management decisions

Baseline Fouling Ratings in MarineStream support risk-based cleaning decisions, invasive-species risk reduction, and cleaner hulls with lower fuel use.

🔁

Monitoring over time

Repeat inspections before/after cleaning or on schedule show change by hull and niche area — building an objective dataset for national policy and reporting.

Spotted a suspect invasive species?

Keep the specimen and photos, note the vessel's recent ports, and report to the Department of Environment (MEIDECC) and Biosecurity authorities.
